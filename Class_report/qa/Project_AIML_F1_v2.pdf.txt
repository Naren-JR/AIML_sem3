Document purpose and project summary
This document consolidates the agreed research direction, states a defensible novelty claim relative to the sources
reviewed so far, and lays out a practical technical plan. It is a project specification—not a claim that the estimator or
optimiser has already been validated.
Project AIML - F1 will use public 2026 Formula 1 telemetry, circuit context, and event-applicable regulations to construct
plausible estimates of one driver's ERS usable-energy state over a race. The initial case study is Kimi Antonelli. The model
starts at Lap 1, time 0.00 s, with an imposed initial condition of 100% of the modelled permitted usable-energy window.
State must be carried continuously across laps; it must not reset to 100% each lap.
Three linked outputs
 State estimation: produce a physically feasible range or ensemble of normalised usable-energy trajectories, not a
claimed direct measurement of battery state.
 Track-position analysis: compare inferred energy behaviour at straights, braking zones, corners, hairpins, and selected
circuit markers.
 Constrained optimisation: use gradient-based optimisation to explore hypothetical deployment schedules under energy,
power, and regulatory constraints, compared against a transparent baseline.
Critical limitation
Public telemetry does not directly provide battery current, voltage, true SoC, or the exact ICE/MGU-K power split. The
hidden energy state is therefore not uniquely identifiable from speed and driver inputs alone. Results must be labelled
estimated/plausible, with assumptions and uncertainty made explicit.
Research question and objectives
Primary question: Given public 2026 F1 telemetry and published vehicle/regulatory constraints, how far can a
physics-informed model estimate a feasible ERS usable-energy trajectory, and how does uncertainty affect track-position
analysis and hypothetical deployment optimisation?
 Audit and preprocess telemetry while preserving irregular timestamps and provenance.
 Build a longitudinal energy model with explicit assumptions for mass, drag, rolling resistance, efficiencies, regeneration,
and power limits.
 Estimate feasible energy trajectories and quantify sensitivity to uncertain parameters.
 Apply gradient descent to a well-defined differentiable objective; report convergence and constraint handling.
 Evaluate using consistency checks, sensitivity, ablations, and baselines—not unsupported ground-truth accuracy.
 Explain commercial transfer potential and what extra data would be required.

1. Novelty: defensible claim
We should not claim this is the first work to estimate battery SoC, use physics-informed models, optimise hybrid energy
management, or study F1 energy strategy. These are established areas. The defensible contribution is a specific integration
and evaluation designed for the limitations of public 2026 F1 telemetry.
Proposed contribution statement
We propose a public-data, physics-informed framework for reconstructing feasible ranges of 2026 F1 ERS
usable-energy state from telemetry that does not expose true battery state or the internal power split. The
framework combines continuous cross-lap energy accounting, event-versioned regulatory constraints,
track-position analysis, uncertainty/sensitivity reporting, and gradient-based optimisation of a hypothetical
deployment policy. The contribution is the integrated, transparent methodology and its evaluation—not direct
measurement of actual car SoC.

2. Cross-referenced literature and evidence
Kleisarchaki (2026), “Opponent State Inference Under Partial Observability: An HMM-POMDP Framework for 2026
Formula 1 Energy Strategy.” The arXiv abstract describes an HMM for hidden rival ERS/tyre states and a DQN policy for
strategic decisions. It reports 92.3% ERS inference accuracy and 95.7% trap recall on synthetic races generated from its
own assumptions; empirical validation is described as planned. This is adjacent F1 strategy work, but it does not establish
real-world battery SoC estimation accuracy. Our target is a single driver's continuous feasible energy trajectory from public
telemetry.
“Driving conditions-driven energy management strategies for hybrid electric vehicles: A review” (Renewable and
Sustainable Energy Reviews, 2021). This review covers energy-management methods driven by on-road driving
conditions, including learning-based and data-driven approaches. It contradicts the blanket claim that commercial hybrid
research ignores real-world conditions. Our proposed distinction is the F1/public-telemetry context and explicit handling of
hidden powertrain variables, rules, and uncertainty.
FIA regulations. The official FIA index lists 2026 Section C Technical Regulations, Issue 21, dated 19 October 2026. For
each analysed race, use the rules applicable to that event and record exact clauses and limits. Do not assume every power
or energy limit is constant across every segment or regulatory update.
Public telemetry and circuit graphics. Available fields include time, speed, throttle, brake, gear, RPM, distance, and
acceleration estimates. Circuit graphics can identify landmarks and certain mode/detection/activation lines, but they do not
prove actual activation or reveal battery power/SoC. The current comparison is based on sources reviewed so far, not a
systematic literature review.
Review conclusion
Position this as a constrained, uncertainty-aware reconstruction and optimisation study under partial observability. Expand
the literature review before submission to include peer-reviewed battery SoC estimation, physics-informed state estimation,
hybrid energy-management optimisation, and F1 telemetry research. For each source, record method, data, ground truth,
validation setting, limitations, and overlap.
3. Commercial-field application
The transferable idea is not the F1 vehicle model or FIA limits. It is the workflow: combine imperfect operating data with a
physics-based energy model, enforce feasible energy/power constraints, quantify uncertainty, and optimise energy use

4. Data and preprocessing plan
Use the public TracingInsights F1 data source discussed in project planning, not FastF1. Preserve original files and create a
reproducible processed dataset with source/version metadata.
Required checks
 Load the complete session/lap sequence from Lap 1 onward. A single-lap JSON cannot initialise Lap 10's state correctly.
 Validate array lengths, finite values, timestamp order, duplicates, missing values, and units.
 Keep actual irregular time steps. Do not silently resample or assume uniform sampling.
 Convert speed from km/h to m/s. Check distance progression against speed-integrated distance.
 Recompute diagnostic acceleration from speed/time and compare it with provided smoothed acceleration; do not remove
strong braking events blindly.
 Treat 'None' in DriverAhead as missing categorical data.
 Align circuit landmarks by distance/lap position, allowing for alignment uncertainty.
 Record session, pit/neutralisation, weather, tyres, track status, and regulation version where available; mark missing
metadata explicitly.

5. Physics model and energy state
Start with a longitudinal road-load model. It is approximate: actual F1 behaviour also includes cornering, aero/downforce
effects on tyres, gear shifts, transients, traffic, and elevation.
v = speed(km/h) / 3.6
a■ ≈ (v■■■ − v■■■) / (t■■■ − t■■■) (diagnostic centred difference using actual timestamps)
F_req = m·a + 0.5·ρ·C_DA·v² + C_rr·m·g (initial level-track model)
P_wheel ≈ v·F_req
Use separate effective aero parameters only if the mode state is supportable. If activation is unknown, treat mode as a
scenario/latent variable. Do not reuse a generation-to-generation drag-reduction figure as the Corner-to-Straight Mode
difference.
Discrete energy balance at a consistently defined electrical interface
E■■■ = E■ − (P_dis,■ / η_d)·Δt■ + η_r·P_rec,■·Δt■
SOC*■ = 100·(E■ − E_min)/(E_max − E_min)
Initial condition: SOC* = 100% at Lap 1, t=0.00 s. This is the top of the modelled permitted usable window, not necessarily
100% of physical electrochemical capacity. The permitted energy swing and other limits must be taken from the
event-applicable FIA rules.
Negative wheel power is not equal to recovered electrical power. Braking energy is divided among drag, rolling resistance,
friction braking, and regeneration. Wheel power also does not reveal the exact ICE/MGU-K contributions. These quantities

6. Estimation and gradient-based optimisation
Separate (A) estimation of feasible latent energy trajectories from (B) optimisation of a hypothetical deployment policy. A low
speed-prediction error does not prove that the inferred battery state is correct.
A. Candidate estimation objective
Let θ contain uncertain physical parameters and latent deployment/recovery variables. A candidate objective may combine
telemetry fit, consistency, smoothness, constraints, and prior assumptions:
J(θ) = λ■·L_speed + λ■·L_accel + λ■·L_smooth + λ■·L_constraints + λ■·L_priors
Define each term only after specifying which quantities the model can predict. Telemetry fit must not dominate latent energy
state without an identifiable link. Priors and smoothness are assumptions; test their influence. Use bounded
parameterisations or projected gradient descent to preserve constraints.
θ■■■ = θ■ − α·∇J(θ■)
If hard mode switches/constraints make the objective non-differentiable, use a differentiable approximation for optimisation
and independently verify the final solution against hard constraints.
B. Hypothetical deployment optimisation
A defensible decision variable is a low-dimensional deployment schedule by track segment rather than a free power value at
every sample. Minimise a stated cost over a chosen horizon, with a terminal energy reserve requirement. Example to refine
after the baseline model works:
min■ J = w■·T_pred(u) + w■·E_used(u) + w■·R_reserve(u) + w■·Σ(u■■■ − u■)²
Subject to E_min ≤ E■ ≤ E_max; 0 ≤ P_deploy,■ ≤ P_max,■; 0 ≤ P_rec,■ ≤ P_rec,max,■; and event-specific
energy, power, and deployment rules.
Here u is a hypothetical policy, T_pred is predicted time or a justified proxy, E_used is energy use, R_reserve penalises
failure to meet the target finish reserve, and the smoothness term discourages unrealistic oscillation. If there is no credible
lap-time model, do not claim to optimise lap time; optimise a transparent proxy and name it accurately.
Evaluation plan
 Compare a fixed/heuristic baseline and the optimised policy under identical assumptions.
 Vary mass, C_DA, rolling resistance, efficiencies, initial state, and power limits across defensible ranges.
 Ablate track segmentation, uncertainty treatment, and selected constraints.
 Test additional laps/sessions/circuits if available; reserve held-out data.
 Audit every constraint violation. A good objective with infeasible controls is not a valid solution.
 Use multiple initialisations; if distinct trajectories fit similarly, report a range.

gradient-based optimisation, and clearly states what cannot be inferred.
